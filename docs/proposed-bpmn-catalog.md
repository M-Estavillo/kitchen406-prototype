# Proposed BPMN Subprocess Catalog for Kitchen406

Source: [Kitchen406 - Capstone Paper, SRS](../Chapters/Kitchen406%20-%20Capstone%20Paper.md), primarily Functional Requirements in SRS Sections 4.1–4.19. NFRs support the process controls; business rules clarify applicable policies.

This revision replaces the earlier broad workflow catalog with **39 bounded subprocess BPMNs**. IDs run from BPMN-01 through BPMN-39. BPMN-10 covers the merged payment and purchase-confirmation workflow. Each row describes a separately drawable process with a trigger, a concrete result, and FR traceability. This document is a diagram inventory, not the BPMN diagrams themselves.

## How the diagrams fit together

The complete standard-order, subscription, and custom-cake journeys are **process maps**, not additional large detailed BPMNs. Each journey links the smaller diagrams below. If an overview BPMN is needed, show only collapsed subprocesses or call activities, the major routing gateways, and start/end events. Keep detailed tasks and exceptions inside the referenced diagrams.

Use a **call activity** when invoking a separately defined reusable process. An embedded collapsed subprocess may be used for local decomposition, but should not be mistaken for a reference to a shared global process. Event-triggered work, such as notification delivery, runs independently and does not require the originating process to await message delivery.

A subprocess should have one business outcome. Do not expand cart editing, fulfillment arrangement, reservations, payment, production, and delivery in one diagram. A useful drawing target is roughly 5–10 main activities per detailed diagram; this is a readability guideline, not an SRS requirement. Split further if the main path or exceptions become hard to follow.

### Journey maps using the subprocess IDs

| Journey | Sequence of separate subprocesses |
| --- | --- |
| Standard order | **01 Cart preparation → 02 Fulfillment arrangement → 09 Purchase reservation → 10 Payment and purchase confirmation → 12 Owner allocation → 13 Production → 14 Pickup OR 15 Courier booking → 16 Courier tracking/completion.** Payment failures and late payments are handled within 10. |
| Subscription enrollment | **03 Subscription selection → 02 Fulfillment arrangement → 09 Purchase reservation → 10 Payment and purchase confirmation → 04 Subscription activation.** The enrollment includes full prepayment and commitments for four fulfillments. |
| Each subscription fulfillment | For the first fulfillment, retain the ingredients reserved at checkout. For later fulfillments, **11 Scheduled ingredient reservation** runs one day before preparation. Once due and materially ready: **12 Owner allocation → 13 Production → 14 Pickup OR 15 Courier booking → 16 Courier tracking/completion; either completion triggers 05 Subscription progress update.** Deferment uses 06 independently before preparation day begins. |
| Custom cake | **07 Request submission → 08 Quotation review/revision/acceptance → 09 Purchase reservation → 10 Payment and purchase confirmation → 12 Owner allocation → 13 Production → 17 Owner delivery/handoff.** Delivery terms are agreed in 08. |

The sequences show successful paths. Each subprocess retains its own unsuccessful outcomes. A stage ending with rejection, unresolved payment, or insufficient stock does not automatically continue to the next stage. Authentication is a precondition for protected transactions; its detailed flow appears only in 26.

## Reuse and handoff rules

- **01** owns standard-cart edits and subtotal calculation; **03** owns subscription offering, variant, quantity and four-week schedule selection. **02** branches by purchase type, collects the applicable fulfillment arrangement, and reviews the full payable total. Item/configuration edits end the current review and return to **01**/**03**; fulfillment edits remain in **02**. Re-entry revalidates dates and applicable address/quotations and recalculates totals. Displaying items again in **02** is purchase review, not another cart-editing process.
- Pickup bypasses **18** for either purchase type and carries no delivery fee. Subscription pickup applies to all four fulfillments; courier subscriptions require one confirmed address and prepaid fees for all four.

- **02 and 18** serve standard orders and subscription enrollment. Cake delivery terms are negotiated in 08; cakes do not pass through courier-distance validation or fee quotation.
- **09 and 10** provide shared purchase reservation, payment, confirmation, and exception handling across the three purchase types. Pass the purchase type, agreed details, saved payable amount, and resource scope as inputs. Do not duplicate these flows inside each intake diagram.
- **09** owns the checkout resource-check/reservation detail. **06** reuses applicable capacity-validation rules for deferment, and the late-payment branch in **10** reuses the original purchase's full validation rules for reconciliation. They do not need to invoke pending-purchase creation or ingredient checkout reservation when those actions do not apply.
- **12 and 13** serve standard orders, each weekly subscription fulfillment, and accepted paid cakes. Product/recipe inputs differ; the operational allocation and preparation controls are shared. A combined allocation/status save must remain atomic despite separate diagrams.
- **15 and 16** are the shared courier processes for standard orders and weekly subscription fulfillments. Booking and tracking are split because they have different triggers and waiting/recovery behavior. An unresolved booking must be reconciled before another booking can safely be attempted.
- **17** is the separate manual delivery process for cakes. Standard and subscription pickup use **14**, as supported by SRS scope items 3 and 6 and FR-FUL-21. Cake pickup is outside this catalog.
- **20 and 21** serve inventory work arising from ordinary maintenance or shortage recovery. Receiving stock may enable **11** to retry; it does not itself confirm the waiting reservation or start production.
- **24** serves confirmation, upcoming subscription, status-change, and other applicable external messages. **25** serves internal operational notices. A message-sending failure does not reverse a valid purchase, reservation, or completion.
- **05** updates subscription progress after pickup or courier completion; it does not repeat payment or the Delivered transition. Retried events must not decrement remaining deliveries twice.

For later drawing, put the input record and output state in data objects or annotations. Connect separate participant pools with message flows; keep sequence flows within a pool. A document link or an arrow in a journey map is not itself a BPMN message flow.

## SRS boundaries and points to clarify before drawing

1. **Subscription review eligibility:** FR-REV-01, 03, and 07 describe a completed purchased order item. BR-21 specifies one subscription review only after all four fulfillments and excludes separate weekly reviews. Mark the subscription branch of 37 for SRS alignment; do not silently allow weekly reviews.
2. **Manual cake delivery:** Transport is outside courier integration, but FR-FUL-21 requires the owner's system handoff confirmation. Diagram 17 includes that explicit system action after the manual work.
3. **Subscription commitments:** For courier subscriptions, BR-26 fixes the address for all four fulfillments after payment; pickup requires no address. BR-27 includes delivery fees in prepayment and assigns later actual-fee differences to the bakery. Do not insert weekly customer payment or automated fee-refund steps into 15/16. Any applicable refund following a later change to pickup is handled manually by owners outside the platform (scope item 6).
4. **Timely cake submission:** FR-CAP-21 requires submission at least seven calendar days ahead. Subsequent review does not invalidate timely submission if fulfillment remains feasible; 09/10 must not blindly reapply the submission deadline as though a new request were being submitted.
5. **Accidental reversal:** FR-FUL-27 supports an authorized reversal but does not fully define eligible states or every stock/unlocking consequence. Diagram 19 must not invent unrestricted reversal or automatic stock restoration.
6. **Marketing:** The inspected SRS FR catalog ends at Section 4.19 and supplies no marketing FR family. Marketing BPMNs need defined functional scope before inclusion despite marketing references elsewhere in the paper.
7. **No inferred extra operations:** Recurring billing, automated supplier purchasing, automated refunds, owner payouts, and live rider-location tracking are outside the supported scope.

Recommended first drawing set: **01, 02, 09, 10, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16** for the standard-order path and its reusable components. Then add subscription- and cake-specific diagrams. The remaining supporting processes can be drawn independently.
